Privacy

Last updated 3 November 2026

My Medical Daybook (Daybook for short) is made and run by Mark 1 Apps Ltd, a company registered in England and Wales (company number 17510025), which is responsible for the information the app holds (the "data controller" in UK law). Mark 1 Apps Ltd is registered with the Information Commissioner's Office, registration number ZC275185. Contact: support@mymedicaldaybook.co.uk.

The short version

  • What you record in Daybook is yours. It lives in your care circle's own locked space. Only the people you choose see it, and you can take anyone out.
  • Mark 1 Apps Ltd never looks at your records in running Daybook. The only exceptions are if you ask us for help and agree to us looking, or where the law requires it.
  • No circle can see another circle's records. The database enforces this, not just the app.
  • Nothing is sold, nothing is used for advertising, and nothing is used to train AI.
  • Your health information leaves your circle's space only when you use a feature that sends it (listed below, each one started by a tap), or through the medicine reminders if you switch them on.
  • You can delete any entry, document or recording yourself, and you can ask for everything to be exported or erased.
  • No cookies, no tracking, no analytics.

Your care circle

A care circle is one patient's records and the people they have let in. It is set up by the patient, or by a carer for someone they look after. A carer setting it up names the patient and confirms that the patient has agreed, or cannot decide this for themselves and the carer is acting for them; that confirmation is kept with the circle, with the date. The records are the patient's, whoever typed them in: the patient's rights below come first, and the patient can take charge of the circle at any time. Only the patient, and the person who set the circle up until they hand it over, can invite someone in or remove them. Everyone else in the circle has exactly the access they were given and nothing more.

What Daybook stores

Your sign-in: your email address and password (held by Google's Firebase Authentication; Daybook never sees the password), your first name, your role in the circle (family, read only, or medicines and treatment only), whether you are the patient or a carer, and which circle you belong to.

What your circle records: check-ins (pain, mood, sleep, sickness, appetite, energy, how much you felt low, worried or enjoyed something each evening, a weekly check whose questions follow the conditions Daybook is set up for, a carer's own weekly check, and their own mood score and notes at the patient's morning and evening times, all on how the patient seemed, with Crohn's or colitis tummy pain, whether your gut stopped you doing anything, nights up for the toilet, whether you are fasting that day and which kind, and a weekly question about joints, eyes, skin and mouth, and what you write), readings (temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen, weight and BMI), food and drink, notes, questions for the care team and their answers, including any voice recordings you choose to make, medicines, doses and reminder times, letters and documents you photograph or paste in and the explanations saved with them, bowel movements (the type, any blood, urgency and accidents, and with Crohn's or colitis your usual number a day and whether you have a stoma), blood test and calprotectin results you type in or read from a photo, as the team gave them, with Crohn's or colitis the flare plan your team gave you, which days you mark as flare days and the calls you log to the advice line, symptoms between check-ins, each night's sleep (when you went to bed and woke, whether you felt rested, whether it was broken, what broke it and roughly for how long), whether you use a watch or health app, which conditions Daybook is set up for, treatment sessions and the calendar, exercise ticks, steps, sleep, workouts and food totals if you connect Apple Health, saved meals, who to contact (names, roles, phone numbers, any extension or menu option, and email addresses), and your settings.

On your phone: a copy of your account record and of the records you have opened, so the app works on a weak signal, and a few settings (which charts you view as tables, the exercise filter, the weight unit, the opener). Words typed into a note or a question are kept on the phone until you save or close that screen, so they are not lost if the phone closes the app; they are cleared when you save, cancel or clear them, and after 7 days at most. A check-in you have started is kept whole (the scores, the words and the step you were on) on the phone and in your circle's space until it is saved or you start it again, 7 days at most, so it comes back as you left it on either phone. When you sign out, Daybook clears its copy of the records, the words kept as typed and the unfinished check-ins from that phone; only settings such as the weight unit stay. Your browser holds these and they go when you clear its data.

If you turn on reminders: each phone's push address and browser type, so the reminder reaches it. For Daybook Assistant: a daily count per circle of how many requests were made and how many words they used, for the cost.

Where it is kept

In Google Cloud's Firestore database in London (the europe-west2 region), through Google's Firebase service and under Google's security measures. Everything travels encrypted (HTTPS), and Google encrypts it when stored. It is not end-to-end encrypted, so Google's systems and the operator can technically reach it. Anything you send with an AI button goes to Anthropic in the United States. The app itself is served from GitHub Pages.

Who can see it

  • The members of your care circle, the people the patient, or whoever set the circle up, chose, according to the access they were given. Family members can add, change and delete. A read-only member sees everything and changes nothing. A "medicines and treatment only" member sees medicines, the treatment calendar (with the mood marks) and the readings, sleep and drinks charts, and nothing else. The database itself enforces these rules, not just the app.
  • Mark 1 Apps Ltd, as the operator, has administrative access to the database in order to run it. Faults are in the code, not in anyone's circle, so running Daybook never involves reading your records. The only times anyone at Mark 1 Apps Ltd would open a circle's records are if you ask for help with something only your own records can explain and agree to it first, or where the law requires it. Both would be rare, and you would know.
  • Usage counts. To see which parts of Daybook are used and keep it running well, the operator sees counts, worked out once a night: how many circles there are, how many logged anything in the last week, how many check-ins were done, how many times each Daybook Assistant feature and the Send by email button were used, and which conditions circles are set up for. Each circle appears as a short code that cannot be traced back to it, with those counts and the date something was last logged. The counts never include anything you wrote, any reading, any name or any address, and the daily totals are kept for 90 days.
  • Google, as the company hosting the database, under its own data processing terms.
  • Cloudflare, which runs Daybook's bridge, Resend, only for a report you email from Daybook, and Anthropic, only for what you send with an AI button, as described below.
  • Nobody else, unless the law requires it.

What leaves your circle's space, and when

  • Send to the team and Send by email, only when you tap Send. The report PDF, your message and the contact's address go through Daybook's bridge to Resend, the email service that sends it, from reporting@mymedicaldaybook.co.uk in your name. Your own sign-in email address is the reply address and gets a copy, so the clinic's reply comes straight to you, never to Mark 1 Apps Ltd. Resend keeps the email in its sending records for a limited time under its own terms; the bridge keeps only a daily count, and your circle keeps a note of what was sent to whom and when, which you can delete. Where Daybook cannot send (the demo, or before it is switched on) the email goes through your own Mail app instead and nothing passes through the bridge.
  • Scan a barcode, or search packaged foods, only when you do it. The camera looks for a barcode on your phone and no picture is kept or sent. Only the barcode number, or the food words you typed in the search box, go through Daybook's bridge to Open Food Facts, a free and open food database, to ask what the product is, and, only when Open Food Facts does not know a barcode, the barcode number alone to FoodData Central, the US Department of Agriculture's free food database; nothing about you goes with them. If the product is found, its name and label figures are saved in your circle's My foods, so they can be used again. The camera is used only while the scanner is open.
  • Daybook Assistant, only when you tap. Daybook Assistant is Daybook's name for its AI helper, which is Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, a US company. Seven features send something through Daybook's bridge to Anthropic for it: Explain with Daybook Assistant (a letter's text or page photos), Suggest questions to ask and Tidy up my notes with Daybook Assistant (the Notes for the team text), Write the brief with Daybook Assistant on two reports (the scores and notes behind the Mood and wellbeing report; the scores, words and doses behind the Pain and pain relief report), Read it from a photo (a photo of a medicine) and Add from a photo or PDF of the plan (an exercise or physio plan). So that information goes to the United States. Anthropic does not use what is sent through its API to train its models and handles it under its own terms. The bridge keeps nothing of what was sent or returned, only a count of words used, for the cost. An explanation of a letter is saved with the letter, and you can change or delete it; the other replies are shown for you to check, and only what you then save is kept. There is a daily limit per circle.
  • Save as PDF, Print, Share and Copy make a copy for you to give to the clinic or someone else. Once it has left the app, it is in your hands.
  • Tap to speak uses your phone's own speech recognition (Apple's on an iPhone, Google's in Chrome). Depending on the phone, the audio may be processed on the phone or on that company's servers under their terms. Daybook receives only the words.
  • Provide feedback (while Daybook is in Beta) sends what you type, any picture you add, which screen you were on, the app version and the kind of phone to Mark 1 Apps Ltd through the bridge, so the app can be put right. It is kept only until the Beta is over, is never part of your circle's records and is read by Mark alone. Never put health details in it unless the problem needs them.
  • Apple Health. Nothing goes to Apple from Daybook, and Daybook never writes to Apple Health. In the iPhone app, if you tap Connect Apple Health and allow it, Daybook reads your steps, sleep, workouts, weight and the day's food energy from Apple Health each time it opens and sends them through Daybook's bridge into your circle's space, where they are kept like anything else you record. You can stop it at any time in the Health app (Sharing, Apps, Daybook). Health data from Apple Health is never used for advertising or anything other than showing it in your Daybook and its reports. If instead you set up the Health Auto Export app to send steps, sleep, workouts, weight and BMI, and food totals, that data comes from your phone to Daybook's bridge and into your circle's space. The bridge keeps the last message it received, trimmed, so a problem with the feed can be checked.
  • Reminders. If you turn on medicine reminders, each notification goes through your browser's push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla). The message is encrypted so that service cannot read it, and unless you switch it off it names no medicine ("A medicine is due"), so no medicine name shows on your lock screen.
  • Fonts and database code. The app loads its fonts from Google Fonts and the database's code from Google. Those servers see your phone's internet address when the app loads, as with any website. None of your entries go to them. Every other code library is kept inside Daybook itself.
  • Daybook's bridge. A small service on Cloudflare that handles sign-up, invites, the Apple Health feed, reminders, the emailed reports and Daybook Assistant's requests. It checks your sign-in, or for the Apple Health feed your circle's inbox key, and writes only your circle's records and your own sign-up record. Cloudflare keeps standard request logs for a short time.

Why the data is held

To provide the service you have asked for, with your explicit consent for the health information you choose to record. UK data protection law calls health information "special category data", which is why it is handled with the care above. You can withdraw consent at any time by deleting what you recorded or asking for it to be erased.

How long

Until you delete it. Deleting an entry, document or recording removes it from the database straight away. Removing a member removes their access and their reminders. The last Apple Health message held by the bridge is overwritten by the next one. Under More, Settings, Your records, the patient (or whoever set the circle up) can download every record and delete the whole circle, which erases everything in it and their sign-in at once; anyone else can leave and delete their own sign-in, and what they logged stays in the patient's records. You can also ask using the contact details at the top and it will be done within 30 days. Google keeps standard server logs for a limited period.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the information held about you, have it corrected, have it erased, have a copy to take elsewhere, and object to how it is used. The copy and the erasing you can do yourself under More, Settings, Your records; for anything else, ask using the contact details at the top. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

A sign-in holder must be 18 or over. A parent or carer may set up a circle and record on behalf of someone they look after, including a child, with that person's agreement where they are able to give it, and otherwise acting in their interests.

The demo

The Guest button opens a shared demo. Everything in it is made up, anyone can see what is added, and it is wiped every night. Put no real details in it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes.
