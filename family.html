Meet us

Mark and Shelley.

My Medical Daybook is made by Mark 1 Apps Ltd, our small family company.

How it began

We met at school, started going out at fourteen, and married in 2004. Mark's Army career took us all over, including years in Germany, then many years in Wiltshire, walking its beautiful countryside, before we settled in Bedfordshire to be nearer our families. For many years our beautiful black labrador, Max, came on all sorts of magical adventures with us. Now our three-year-old daughter brings us a lot of joy, and the adventures carry on for as long as we can.

Shelley brings the calm; Mark brings the storm. Daybook is what came of the two together: something to make whatever people are going through a little easier, by helping them stay organised and keep a record that helps their doctors and nurses understand what's really going on.

Our Ambassadors

The people who tell the next person.

Daybook spreads the way it started: one person showing another. Our ambassadors are patients, carers, nurses, therapists and support group leaders who believe in it and help it reach the people who need it, in a clinic, a group, online or over a cup of tea.

What an ambassador does

Tells people about Daybook in their own way: a word in a support group, a leaflet in a clinic, a post online. We give you what you need, and you tell us what people say, so we can make it better.

The story in brief

In August 2026, at 44, Mark Brown was told he had stage 4 pancreatic cancer. Between appointments the questions came at all hours and were gone by the time he saw his team, so he and his wife Shelley built My Medical Daybook: a private health diary shared by a care circle, the patient and the people they choose.

Daybook keeps the medicines, symptoms, food, sleep and questions from the days in between, and turns them into a short report a nurse or doctor can read in a minute. It now helps people with cancer, Crohn's and colitis, and any long-term condition. It gives no medical advice and never will.
